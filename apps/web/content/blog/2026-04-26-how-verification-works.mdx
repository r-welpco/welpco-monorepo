There's a thing that happens on a lot of marketplaces. You see a "Verified" badge next to a profile, and you assume it means something. Then you find out the verification was an automatic email confirmation and the badge gets handed out to anyone who signs up. The word stops meaning anything, but it keeps showing up — on every listing — because removing it would look like a downgrade.

We don't want to do that.

## What the badge means on Welpco

The badge on a Welper's profile means one thing: **a third-party criminal record check came back clear.**

It's a Basic Canadian criminal record check, run by Certn, our screening partner. Certn confirms the Welper's identity as part of that screening and searches the criminal record databases available to them. When the result comes back clear, the badge appears on the profile. That's the whole claim.

It does **not** mean we've rated them five stars. It does not mean they're insured. It does not mean we've assessed how good they are at the work, or endorsed them. We're trying very hard to keep the badge to one job, so that when you see it you know exactly what you're being told.

## Why we don't hand it out at signup

You can probably guess. If we marked every account "verified" the second they signed up, two things would happen. The badge would stop being a useful filter — every Welper would have it, so no one would. And the moment someone faked their way through, we'd be shipping a label that misled you instead of helping you. The only thing worse than no verification is verification that lies to you.

Some of this is in the bible we keep for ourselves. The short version: a five-star average from one review is noise, not signal. A verified badge that everyone has is decoration, not data. We'd rather show "No reviews yet" honestly than fake a number we don't have — and we'd rather leave the badge off, visibly, than print a green checkmark nobody earned.

## The check is optional, and we say so

Here's the part most marketplaces would bury: **the background check is optional.** A Welper who skips it can still build a profile, appear in search, and take bookings. They just don't get the badge.

We went back and forth on this. Requiring it would make for a cleaner story, but it would also price out people who do good work — a one-time fee is a real barrier when you're just starting out — and it would tell you less than you'd think, because a clear record isn't a promise about how someone behaves in your home. So we made it optional, and we made the absence of it visible instead of hiding it.

If a Welper wants the badge: it's a one-time fee (currently $19.99 CAD plus tax), paid in their dashboard. Certn emails them a screening link, results usually land within a few business days, and the badge appears on the profile once the check comes back clear. If it doesn't come back clear, the profile stays active — there's just no badge.

## How to read it, if you're a customer

Every Welper card and profile shows the badge in one of two states, and both are on screen:

- **Green shield with a check** — the background check passed.
- **Grey shield with an x** — no verification badge.

The grey state is doing real work, so read it carefully. It means only that we do not have a passed result on file. It does not disclose whether a check was never started, is still in progress, or ended without a passed result. If a passed check matters for the job you're booking — someone in your home, around your kids, alone with your pets — you can filter search to show only Welpers who've passed.

A few other things that are real, and that we'd rather point at than dress up as "verification":

- **Portfolio photos are reviewed by a person** before they go public, so what you're looking at isn't an unmoderated upload.
- **Reviews go both ways** after a booking. Customers review Welpers, Welpers review customers, and the rating you see on a profile comes from customers.
- **Messaging and payments stay on the platform**, which is what lets us actually help when something goes wrong.
- **Every account confirms its email** with a code before it can transact.

## What we're still honest about

A few things aren't solved, and we'd rather say it here than let you find out later:

- **Insurance.** Welpco doesn't insure the work. Some Welpers carry their own liability insurance and mention it on their profile. We're working on a clearer way to surface that, and on a Welpco-side option for the categories where it matters most.
- **A clear record is not a character reference.** It tells you what a database has on someone. It doesn't tell you whether they'll show up on time or be careful with your things. Reviews do more of that work, and they take time to accumulate.
- **In-person trust.** Verification happens in software. The actual trust between you and a Welper happens when you meet them. Read messages carefully, ask questions, and start small if the work is significant.

We'd rather under-promise on this stuff than oversell it.

## Why the grey x stays

We could show the badge only when it's earned and show nothing otherwise. Plenty of products do exactly that, and it looks tidier. It also quietly lets you assume the check happened.

So the missing state gets a mark of its own. The badge gets to mean something only if we're willing to show you, plainly, when it isn't there.
